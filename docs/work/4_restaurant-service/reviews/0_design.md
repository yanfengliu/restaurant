## Reports

### Codex independent design and effort reviewer

**F0 — Medium: keeping the legacy sink purchasable is a gameplay dependency, so hiding it cannot be left as a free interface choice.** Draft line 27 leaves the old sink's catalog availability to the interface, while line 29 excludes new recipe policy. Outside the draft, `src/catalog.js:50` requires `sink` for Chef's Tasting Plate; `src/engine.js:165-166` and `src/engine.js:613-616` enforce the required equipment. Hiding all sink construction makes that recipe unavailable from a new empty game, even when its other unlock, stock and equipment requirements are met. Saved sinks remaining valid does not help that new-game path. The smallest M4 decision is to keep the existing sink catalog entry and recipe dependency. Any eventual removal needs an explicit gameplay decision and corresponding recipe/equipment acceptance, rather than an interface-only implementation choice. The furnished map also retains its second sink at `src/engine.js:104`, so replacing only the kitchen sink must not be described as eliminating sink washing everywhere.

The proposed money transition is coherent. Current version 2 accepts `wageAccrued` up to `monthlyWage + 0.00001` independently of day phase (`src/engine.js:1203`). Its calendar portion already accrues at monthly salary divided by 450 seconds and pays the accumulated liability (`src/engine.js:752-770`); dismissal pays the same liability (`src/engine.js:860-867`). Preserving cash, historical expenses and the complete unpaid amount on load, then paying once at the next daily boundary or earlier dismissal, preserves that accepted contract without an inferred arrears migration marker. Changing the settlement interval is sufficient; reconstructing employment dates or retroactive daily expenses would add unsupported state and duplicate-charge risk.

The phase-aware validation ceiling in draft line 19 is necessary if accepted saves must remain saveable after time advances. Let salary be M, current day phase p, and accrued balance A no greater than M + Mp/450 plus tolerance. Before the next boundary, adding duration d adds Md/450 to both A and its ceiling. At settlement the debt becomes zero and fits the reset phase. A fixed cap of M plus one daily installment instead accepts a value at its cap and rejects the same save after the next positive accrual, so it merely moves the edge. The implementation should exercise the *new* phase ceiling as well as literal old balances: load the maximum accepted balance at phases zero, midpoint and just before rollover, tick, then round-trip and compare future twins. The existing maximum and zero-wage tolerances should remain consistent. The draft's tests already supply the appropriate scope; no precision library or schema bump is justified.

Version 1 handling is correctly frozen. `src/engine.js:1268-1273` derives the new calendar, maps old wages by 22.5 and sets unpaid liability to zero. Existing literal fixtures check historical cash and statistics (`tests/supplies.test.js:239-250`). The daily-pay change must continue accruing only future elapsed time, without inferring past unpaid days. Paying a legacy version 2 balance on the first future boundary can be much larger than the normal displayed daily estimate; it should remain visibly identified as accrued pay so the transition does not look like an unexplained full-month daily charge. This does not require a second persistent liability field.

The equipment boundary is appropriately conservative. A visible 0/5 indicator establishes neither five concurrent workers nor a batch threshold, unattended cycle, clean output buffer, retrieval owner or timing. Draft lines 31-37 leave these choices open instead of disguising the old single-dish sink as a five-slot appliance. Existing validation couples washing to an employee (`src/engine.js:1220-1225` and `src/engine.js:1248-1251`); a confirmed unattended cycle would require a real new owner/progress alternative, not simply another allowed object type. Conversely, no new owner graph should be implemented before that observation. Separate `washCapacity` from storage capacity because storage/reservations are checked through `ITEMS[object.type].capacity` (`src/engine.js:1253`). Preserve the current sink graph and per-type conservation ledger (`src/engine.js:1254`) throughout.

The highest-value remaining native probe is a complete minimal service cycle, followed by one day boundary with the hired worker and explicit cash/expense readings. One completed meal supplies a dirty dish and demonstrates that staff, menu, arrivals and service are active; its pickup, washer occupancy, employee departure or continued attendance, and clean retrieval distinguish the competing appliance models. Record fewer, decisive transitions instead of more idle date checkpoints. The coordinator's Jan 5–26 salary trace had a hired worker but no food/table/menu flow, so its flat cash does not yet distinguish payment cadence from scenario activation or another prerequisite. The current month-end check is a reasonable final calendar-only discriminator; if flat, switch conditions rather than repeat it. This is a proposed probe, not an observation made by this reviewer.

Published daily-pay evidence at `docs/research.md:125` is stronger than the previous monthly assumption, but the installed build's live wage debit remains unverified. If the bounded service trace is still unavailable, the coordinator may explicitly choose the published daily rule as the implementation target while retaining that uncertainty, and defer the appliance behavior as draft line 37 permits. Do not characterize an unresolved installed-build difference as a verified native match. That decision belongs in the agreed contract and research conclusions before implementation.

The milestone is proportionate if the next step is that bounded service probe and a recoverable contract. It would become a loop if more snapshots of unchanged idle cash were treated as stronger behavioral evidence, or if a general machine scheduler were designed before a single observed wash transition. The current architecture can support either a narrow daily-pay increment or an evidence-backed appliance extension without unrelated recipe expansion, monthly calendar changes or new scenarios. Browser input, pixel/gallery inspection and exact integrated review remain necessary after implementation; this report cannot substitute for them.
